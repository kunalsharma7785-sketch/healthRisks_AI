Why Recall and ROC-AUC Matter More Than Accuracy in Screening

In medical screening, missing a high-risk individual (a False Negative) has catastrophic consequences, whereas a False Positive simply leads to a follow-up test with a physician. Raw accuracy can be dangerously misleading in unbalanced healthcare data (for example, a model that classifies everyone as "healthy" could show 80% accuracy while failing 100% of sick patients).

Consequently, HealthRisk AI optimizes for Sensitivity (Recall) and ROC-AUC (Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve) to guarantee strong discrimination capability across diagnostic thresholds.

About the Datasets & Honest Limitations

Transparent clinical data disclosures and population representation constraints.

Diabetes Cohort

Pima Indians Diabetes Dataset

Collected by the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK). Contains 768 female patient observations of Akimel O'odham (Pima) descent living near Phoenix, Arizona.

  • Sample Size: 768 individuals
  • Missing Data: Zero values recorded for glucose, BP, skinfold, and BMI (handled via median imputation).
Cardiovascular Cohort

Cleveland Heart Disease Dataset

Derived from the UCI Machine Learning Repository, compiled by the Cleveland Clinic Foundation. Contains 303 patient records undergoing coronary angiography.

  • Sample Size: 303 individuals
  • Outcome: Angiographic disease status (>50% diameter narrowing in major blood vessels).

Important Scientific Limitations

These benchmark research datasets are relatively small by modern biomedical standards and are not representative of all age groups, ethnicities, or global demographics. Genetic, socioeconomic, and lifestyle variations mean that predictions cannot replace comprehensive diagnostic laboratory panels conducted under clinical supervision.
